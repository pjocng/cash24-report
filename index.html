Cash24 Secured P2P Lending Platform

Regulatory and Build Report · Prepared by Pjoc Nigeria Limited · 15 November 2026

2 approvals to launch Never hold users' money Cars first, land later Under ₦2m official fees

Bottom line

This can be built and launched lawfully with two approvals, a state moneylender licence and FCCPC digital lending approval, provided the platform never holds users' money itself.

What it is in law

Secured money lending delivered through an app. Nigeria has no peer-to-peer lending licence, so the rules that apply are the state Moneylenders Laws, the FCCPC's 2025 digital lending regulations, and collateral law.

What to avoid

Three features pull in heavy licences: a stored-value wallet (CBN mobile money, ₦2bn), taking money from the public to on-lend (banking and deposit rules), and pooling creditors into shared investments (SEC). All three can be designed out.

Recommended structure

Cash24 is the licensed party on every loan. It vets requestors, arranges the loan between one creditor and one requestor, holds and registers the collateral for the creditor, and services repayments. Money moves through a licensed payment partner straight between bank accounts.

Collateral

Start with cars, registered at the National Collateral Registry on every loan. Add landed property later, because a valid mortgage needs Governor's consent and enforcement is slow.

Start with

Counsel's opinion on the structure, the Lagos moneylender licence, FCCPC approval, SCUML and data protection registrations, and a payment partner. Official fees are under ₦2m in total.

Add later

Partner bank accounts if creditors want held balances, then a CBN finance company or microfinance bank licence once volume justifies ₦100m or more in capital.

Main open pointWhether individual creditors who lend repeatedly need their own moneylender licence. This needs a written opinion from a Nigerian fintech lawyer before the build starts.

The model and where Cash24 stands today

A secured peer-to-peer lending marketplace: creditors fund vetted requestors, who pledge a car or landed property and repay through the platform.

Points that shape the design

Creditors may themselves count as moneylenders
Under the Lagos law, a person who lends at interest is presumed to be a moneylender. A creditor who lends repeatedly through the platform risks being an unlicensed lender, which can make the loan unenforceable. This is the biggest legal weakness of a pure peer-to-peer design.
The FCCPC rules are in force
A court suspended them in April 2026, then upheld them on 20 July 2026, and the FCCPC resumed enforcement (Mondaq). Unapproved lenders are removed from the public register.
Unlicensed deposit-taking is a criminal offence
BOFIA 2020 sets a minimum of five years' imprisonment and a penalty of at least ₦50m or twice the amount collected (Mondaq). The platform must never hold creditor money as its own.
The SEC crowdfunding route does not fit
Those rules cover companies raising money, limited to MSMEs with a two-year track record, not loans to individuals (Mondaq).
A CBN-licensed lender is treated differently by the FCCPC
Microfinance banks and other CBN-licensed institutions apply for a waiver from registration but must still follow the conduct rules (Jackson, Etti & Edu).

Why B works with fewer licences

  • Cash24 never takes deposits. A creditor's money moves only when they commit to a specific loan, and repayments pass straight through.
  • No pooling and no promised return, which keeps the product away from SEC fund and crowdfunding rules.
  • A licensed entity is party to every loan, so consumer protection duties and collateral registration have a clear owner.

Design rules that keep B inside the lines

  • One creditor per loan at launch.
  • Creditors onboarded privately and verified. No public advertising of returns.
  • Cash24 earns fees (arrangement, servicing, valuation), not an interest spread on creditor money.
  • Cash24 does not guarantee repayment. The creditor's protection is the collateral.
  • Frequent creditors move to a formal arrangement counsel approves, for example lending through their own licensed company.

Collateral: cars and landed property

Holding a car or a title document is not enough on its own. The security must be documented and registered, or it can fail against other creditors and in court.

What custody does

Recommended practice

  • Verify ownership, customs papers and any existing lien before approval. Search the National Collateral Registry for prior interests.
  • Sign a security agreement describing the vehicle by chassis and engine number, with consent to repossession on default.
  • Register the interest at the National Collateral Registry on the day of disbursement.
  • Hold original vehicle documents and a spare key. Store the car in an insured yard, or let the borrower keep driving with a tracker fitted.
  • Take insurance with the security holder noted as first loss payee.

Recommended practice

  • Accept only titled property: a Certificate of Occupancy or registered deed. Run a land registry search and a physical inspection on every file.
  • For smaller or shorter loans, use an equitable mortgage: deposit of title deeds, a memorandum of deposit, and a signed deed of legal mortgage held for later perfection. Counsel should confirm the form for each state.
  • For larger loans, perfect a full legal mortgage and price the consent and registration cost into the loan.
  • Expect slow enforcement. Loan-to-value limits should reflect that.

Custody and the Pawnbrokers Law

Lending against goods kept in the lender's custody is pawnbroking, which Lagos regulates under its own Pawnbrokers Law with a separate licence (Mondaq). Counsel should confirm whether keeping pledged cars in a Cash24 yard triggers it. Registered security with borrower possession and a tracker avoids the question.

Who holds the securityCash24, or a subsidiary set up for the purpose, should hold all security as trustee for the funding creditor. This keeps one registered secured party per loan and lets enforcement run through one experienced team.

Wallet and money flow

The platform should show wallet balances on screen without ever being a wallet issuer. A real stored-value wallet needs a Mobile Money Operator licence with ₦2bn capital (Nigeria Communications Week).

The design that avoids itEvery naira sits in an account at a licensed bank or payment provider. The app keeps a ledger of who is owed what and instructs the partner to move money. Users see a balance, but it is a record of money in transit for a specific loan, not stored value.

Flow for one loan

Cash24 runs the ledger and instructions. It never holds the funds.

  1. A creditor accepts a request. The app issues a dedicated virtual account number from the licensed payment partner.
  2. The creditor transfers the loan amount from a bank account in their own name. The partner confirms receipt.
  3. Funds are held in a segregated escrow account until collateral checks and registration are complete.
  4. The partner pays the requestor's verified bank account. Cash24's fee is split off at this point.
  5. Each repayment goes to a virtual account tied to that loan.
  6. The repayment is paid out to the creditor's bank account automatically, same or next day, less the servicing fee.
  7. On final repayment, the security is released and the registry entry is discharged.

Rules that keep this outside CBN wallet and deposit rules

  • No free-standing balances. A creditor cannot top up in advance and leave money idle on the platform.
  • No transfers between users other than the loan and its repayment.
  • Payouts only to a bank account whose name and BVN match the verified user.
  • Customer money is never mixed with Cash24's operating account, and Cash24 never uses it.
  • Daily reconciliation of the ledger against the partner's account statement.
Data residencyThe CBN requires payments transaction data generated in Nigeria to be stored in Nigeria, with full compliance by 1 January 2027 (PwC). Host the ledger and transaction records in-country from the start.

Phased roadmap

Start with the two lending approvals and a payment partner, and add the heavier licences only when a specific feature needs them.

Can safely wait
  • A wallet or mobile money licence. The pass-through design removes the need.
  • An SEC registration. Only relevant if creditors become pooled investors.
  • A CBN lending licence. A state licence plus FCCPC approval supports the pilot and early growth.
Cannot wait
  • The moneylender licence and FCCPC approval. Lending through an app without them is an offence.
  • Proper security documents and registry filings on every loan.
  • KYC, data protection and customer fund segregation.
TimingThe FCCPC rules allow at least 30 days for review (Balogun Harold), so both applications should be filed while the app is being built.
